Nervous about walking in? A gathering is a small group of people doing one thing together for a while: a sit, a walk, a workshop, a meal, whatever that Circle is built around. There is a Host, there is an activity, and there is time to talk. No performance, no pressure to be interesting, no one putting you on the spot.

## Get past the first five minutes

The first five minutes are a little awkward. Then the activity takes over and it gets easy. You are not walking into a room of strangers expected to make small talk from nothing. There is a thing to do, and the thing gives everyone something to point at besides each other.

If you are nervous, you are not the only one. Most people in the room felt the same before their first gathering. Showing up at all is the whole ask.

## Know the shape of it

A typical gathering runs in three rough parts:

1. **Arrive and settle.** People trickle in, say hello, find a seat. The Host usually opens with a quick welcome and maybe a one-line round so everyone has said something. Low stakes, short.
2. **The thing itself.** The reason you all came: the practice, the walk, the discussion, the project. This is most of the time, and it does the social work for you. You are doing something side by side, not facing off.
3. **Wind down.** A little unstructured time at the end to talk, swap numbers if you click with someone, and find out when the next one is.

Times vary by Circle. The gathering's page shows when it starts and ends.

## Pack light

Usually you need nothing. The gathering's page lists anything specific, like a mat, water, or a dish to share. When in doubt, bring yourself and show up a few minutes early.

## Come even if you know no one

That is the normal starting point, and the Host knows it. A good Host watches for the new face and folds you in. You do not have to introduce yourself to the whole room or explain why you are there. Stand near the activity, do the thing, and the conversations happen on their own.

## Questions people ask

### Do I have to talk a lot?

No. You can show up, do the activity, and leave having said very little, and that counts as a great first gathering. The talking gets easier the second and third time, when the faces are familiar. Same people, same time, repeated, is how a hello turns into a friendship.

### How early should I get there?

A few minutes early beats right on time. You get to settle and say hi before things start. If you are running late, come anyway. Late is better than not at all.

### Can I bring a friend who is not on Frequency?

Yes, and it is a good move. Bringing one person you already know makes the first five minutes easier for both of you. Share your invite link for the gathering and they can RSVP without an account. See [how do I RSVP](/help/getting-started/how-to-rsvp).

### Can I leave early if it is not for me?

Yes. Slip out whenever you need to. Nobody keeps score. If the Circle is not your fit, try another one. The point is to find your people, and that sometimes takes a couple of tries.
