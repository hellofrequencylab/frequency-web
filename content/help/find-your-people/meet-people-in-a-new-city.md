You moved. The boxes are unpacked, the job is fine, and on a Friday night you
realize you do not have a single person to call. Moving somewhere new and knowing
no one is one of the loneliest stretches of adult life, and it is completely normal
to feel unmoored in it. The good news is that a new city is also a good place to
start fresh, because nobody has a fixed idea of you yet.

## How do I meet people in a new city?

Pick one recurring thing and become a regular at it fast. Do not try to meet the
whole city. Find one small group that meets on a schedule and show up to it twice
before you decide anything.

The first weeks are about putting yourself in the same room more than once:

- Choose recurring over one-off. A weekly group usually beats a one-time event,
  because friendship needs repeats, and a new city gives you no repeats by default.
  You have to manufacture them.
- Show up twice before you judge it. The first time anywhere new is awkward for
  everyone. The second time is when faces start to feel familiar. A lot of people
  quit after one and conclude the city is cold.
- Lead with the activity, not with making friends. It is far easier to walk in for
  a thing to do than to walk in to make friends. The friends come as a side effect.

## Why is it so hard to make friends in a new town?

Because you arrive with zero of the repeated contact that friendship runs on. Back
home you had years of small overlaps: the same gym, the same neighbors, the same
faces at the same places. A new city wipes all of that to zero on day one. It is not
that the people are unfriendly. You just have not been in the same room as anyone
twice yet.

That gap often closes faster than it feels like it will, but only if you build the
repeats on purpose.

## What do I do first?

1. Find one Circle near your new place. A Circle is a small local group, and many
   meet in person on a standing schedule, which is the recurring repeat a new city
   does not hand you for free. Open [Circles](/circles) and look for the ones with
   a location near you, or read how to
   [find a Circle near you](/help/getting-started/join-a-circle).
2. Commit to the next two gatherings before you decide anything.
3. Lower the bar for showing up. The Expression practices are the ones about
   putting yourself out there in small, doable ways: introducing yourself, inviting
   one person, saying the thing. They make walking into a new room less of a cold
   start. [See the Expression practices](/discover/practices/pillar/expression)

No Circle near you yet? Check [Events](/events) for something this week, or read
[Start with something to do](/help/find-your-people/start-with-something-to-do).

You do not have to rebuild a whole social life this month. You have to show up twice to
one thing. That is how a new city stops being a place you live and starts being home.

## Questions people ask

### How long does it take to feel at home in a new city?

Longer than the move and sooner than you fear. For a lot of people it is a matter of
months of regular contact, not years, and it is different for everyone. The clock
starts when you become a regular somewhere, not when the lease starts. Joining one
recurring group early tends to help more than waiting to "get around to it."

### What's the best way to meet people if I work from home?

Build the contact your commute and office used to provide. With no workplace handing you
faces, a standing weekly group is not a nice-to-have, it is likely the main way you will
meet people. Put one recurring thing in your week and protect it like a meeting.

### I'm an introvert and new in town. Where do I even start?

Start with one small group built around something you already like, and give yourself
permission to just attend, not perform. You do not need to work the room. You need to be
in the same small room twice. Quiet regulars become known too.
