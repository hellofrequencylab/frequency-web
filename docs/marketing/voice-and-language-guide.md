## 1. What we are saying

Frequency is a collective where people boldly create together in a meaningful space of love. We help community builders, coaches, guides, healers, teachers, yogis and mindful people gather, collaborate and build circles, not walls, toward a better world to live in together.

It is not about filling a room. It is about meaningful connection. Marketing copy talks to a person who cares about people, not to someone managing a business.

## 2. Two lines, two jobs

| Line | Job | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Build circles, not walls.** | The idea. The stance. Bold and inviting. | Hero and section headlines, about, the story, social. |
| **Get people together. Do things on purpose.** | The tagline. Plain and repeatable. | Site footer, email footer, sign-offs. |

Proposed pairing, to confirm: the footer carries "Get people together. Do things on purpose." and "Build circles, not walls" is the idea the content keeps returning to. Either can swap.

## 3. Tone

Bold and inspiring, like the new way to do things. Inviting and casual, like a friend who hosts. Grounded in specifics (a Thursday walk, a potluck, a folding chair). Any yogi or spiritual person should feel at home; a skeptic should not flinch.

- **Bold:** short declarative sentences. Say the thing. "Build circles, not walls."
- **Inviting:** "you," "we," "come as you are." Never "users," "leads," "customers" in marketing.
- **Love, quietly:** love is allowed as a plain word ("a space of love," "people who love what they do"). It is never decorated.
- **Casual:** contractions, plain verbs. Sentence case. No em dashes.

## 4. The word "community"

Use it. It is what people search for and what our audience calls themselves. Rules:

1. **Put it where search and meaning meet:** page titles, H2s, meta descriptions, first 100 words. Intent phrases worth owning: *build community, how to build a community, community events near me, find your community, spiritual community, wellness community, start a community group, community builder.* The head term alone is crowded and vague (software, local government), so pair it with a place, a practice or an action.
2. **Pair it with something concrete** in the same sentence (a place, a person, an activity). "Community" never carries a headline alone.
3. **Prefer the doing form:** *community builders, building community, gathering.* Fewer "our community" boilerplate lines.
4. Also keep **circle, gather, neighbors, belonging, together** in rotation so the page does not repeat one word.

Research note: web searches returned no usable volume figures, so the intent phrases above come from existing pages and judgement. Confirm in Search Console (already verified) before betting a headline on one.

## 5. Spiritual register: grounded

**Welcome:** gather, practice, heart, belonging, love, presence, ritual (when real), circle, together, meaningful, intention, purpose, rooted.
**Avoid on the surface:** energy, vibration, sacred, manifest, awaken, activate, hold space, tap into, drop into, tribe, high vibe, unlock, elevate, transform your life. Deep pages may use practice vocabulary after a plain entry.

Test: a yoga teacher nods, and a neighbor who has never done yoga does not roll their eyes.

## 7. Features as story

Name the feature only inside the story of what it lets someone do.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Contacts holds every guest, a list you keep | Remember everyone who walked in, and welcome them back by name. |
| Check-in | Greet people at the door and know who came. |
| One room, one list | Everyone who gathers with you, in one place. |
| Run the studio, not the back office | Spend your time with your people, not your paperwork. |
| Everything you run, circling you | Your sessions, your people and your payments, all in one place. |
| Bookings + CRM | Let people sign up, pay and come back, without the chasing. |

Product names (Space, Crew, Circle, Zaps) stay as in [`NAMING.md`](../NAMING.md). A technical word is fine when it is part of the story.

## 8. Never

"Fill the room," "full house," "packed" as the goal. Revenue-first openers. "Software for..." as a headline. Narrated feelings. Hype words. Em dashes.

## 9. Review checklist

1. Does the first line name a person, not a function?
2. Is there one concrete image (a chair, a walk, a Thursday)?
3. Could a yogi and a skeptic both read it aloud?
4. Is "community" next to something real?
5. Is every feature told as what someone gets to do?
6. No filler, no em dashes, sentence case.
