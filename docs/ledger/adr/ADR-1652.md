## ADR-1652: Each seller marks their own share of a split order sent, on their transfer row, and the order reads shipped only once every seller has shipped (LIVE-705)

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-09-29 · `LIVE-705` (filed by the HYG-138 replan from ADR-1616 "Not here") · builds on [ADR-1575](DECISIONS.md) (the fulfilment writer and its three rules), [ADR-1614](DECISIONS.md) (the transfer ledger) and [ADR-1616](DECISIONS.md) (a seller's view of a split order, whose §3 made the split-order control read-only and is amended here) · numbered 1652 as reassigned by the orchestrator after 1640 was taken on main

**Context.** Re-tested on LIVE-624's head (8cf1c86f6) before writing (ADR-1082): `setOrderFulfillment` reads the order with `.eq(scopeColumn, scopeValue)` from `sellerScope` (owner_space_id, owner_profile_id, or owner_kind platform). A split order (owner_kind `split`, funds_flow `separate`) leaves both owner ids null, so every seller it pays read "That order is not one of yours." and a paid split order of physical goods could never be marked sent. LIVE-624 therefore rendered its control read-only. One status per order is also the wrong shape: each seller ships their own lines, from their own place, on their own day.

**Decision.**

1. **Fulfilment of a split order is per seller, on the seller's transfer row.** Migration `20270345010500` adds `fulfillment_status` (the same five-step ladder and CHECK as `commerce_orders`, default `none`) and `fulfilment` (jsonb object: carrier, tracking, note, step times, the record `shipping.fulfilment` holds for a single-seller order) to `commerce_order_transfers`. The row is already each seller's only claim on a split order (ADR-1616 §1), so no second table and no per-line column. The table stays service role only. A per-seller map inside `commerce_orders.shipping` was rejected: two sellers writing one jsonb read-modify-write lose each other's step, while a row each gives each seller their own compare-and-set.
2. **The writer finds a split order through the seller's row.** `setOrderFulfillment` tries the owner-column scope first, exactly as before, so a single-seller order never reads the ledger. Only when that finds nothing, and the seller is a Space or a maker, does it look for a transfer row on that order matched on `owner_kind` AND that kind's owner id (a Space's row never answers to the profile that owns the Space). The Store door (`platform`) never reaches a split order. The step moves forward only, compare-and-set on the step read, and `updated_at` is left alone (it is the reconciler's clock for a transfer not yet landed). A share whose lines need nothing sent is refused ("Nothing in this share needs sending."), as is a share reversed or cancelled, and every order-state refusal of ADR-1575 applies unchanged.
3. **An operator marks any share by naming it.** A new seller kind `{ kind: 'operator', shareId }` reaches exactly the row named, and only on the order named. `setShareFulfillmentAction` (requireOperator) is its door, one per share, on `/admin/marketplace/orders`, labelled with the seller's name.
4. **The order's own status is a roll-up, forward only.** After a share moves, the order's `fulfillment_status` becomes the least advanced step among the shares that have something to send (`rollupShareFulfilment`); a share with only services, bookings, tickets or Journeys, and a share paid back, does not hold it back. The write lands only on an order still at an earlier step, so two sellers finishing together can never pull it back. ADR-1575 rule 3 fires on the roll-up: the order flips `paid` to `fulfilled` once every such share is delivered or complete, which is the state every earnings read already counts. A failed roll-up is logged and the share's step stands; the next share move rolls it up again. The order's own `shipping` is not touched.
5. **The buyer hears once per share, from the seller that shipped.** The shipped notice (`order.shipped`) names that seller and lists their lines only. `/orders` shows each seller's step and tracking under their own lines (`sellerFulfilments`, read with the seller and the step only: no row id and no money figure), and falls back to the order's roll-up if the shares cannot be read.
6. **Every seller surface gets its door (amends ADR-1616 §3).** A seller's view of a split order (`sellerViewOfSplitOrder`) now carries their share's step and record, not the roll-up, so the Space Shop Orders tab and the maker console render the ordinary control with its next step. Staff preview stays read-only.

**Rejected.** One status per split order moved by whichever seller clicks (a seller would mark the other seller's goods sent). A per-line status (a seller ships a share, not a line, and the line has no seller claim of its own). The per-seller jsonb map on the order (point 1). Letting the operator's existing Store door reach a split order (it would have to guess which share). Rolling the order up to the most advanced share (the buyer would read "shipped" while a parcel has not left).

**Consequences.** A paid split order can now be sent, share by share, by the sellers it pays, and closes as fulfilled when the last of them delivers. A seller whose share's fee is its whole gross has no transfer row (ADR-1614 §2), so they neither see nor move the order; that case sends nothing today. Single-seller orders are unchanged, byte for byte on their path. The migration is authored only; the orchestrator applies it before this change merges, and until then the seller reads of a split order fail safe to the owned orders (ADR-1616 §1), since they now select the new columns.

**Rows.** `LIVE-705` (closed here). `LIVE-706` untouched.
