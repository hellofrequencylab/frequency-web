## ADR-1679: A Circle carries one to three Channels, in a join capped by position, with the primary still on the column the feed reads (LIVE-666)

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-09-30 · backlog `LIVE-666` (closed here), `LIVE-730` (filed parked here) · re-tested first per [ADR-1082](DECISIONS.md) · extends [ADR-871](DECISIONS.md) (a Circle declares its Channel) · owner rulings recorded by [ADR-1624](DECISIONS.md) ("Up to 3 Channels", 2026-09-29) and 2026-09-30 ~02:10Z ("Phase it") · migration `20270345011400_circle_channels.sql`

**Context.** A Circle could declare exactly one Channel: the nullable FK `circles.topical_channel_id`. The owner ruled that a Circle may carry up to three. Re-tested on `main` at `30975a1b0`: the column is still the only link, and the one join that ever allowed several, `circle_topics` (20260604010000), was dropped as orphaned in 20260925000000 and is not in the generated types. So this needed a schema change. The column is read in two very different places. Discovery and the Channel pages read it in app code. The feed decides who sees a Circle's cluster posts in the database, in the posts read policy and two feed RPCs. On 2026-09-30 the owner ruled "Phase it": the join and the app readers ship now, and the feed and RLS arms wait.

**Decision.**

1. **A join, capped by its own shape.** `circle_channels (circle_id, topical_channel_id, position)` with `position between 1 and 3`, `primary key (circle_id, topical_channel_id)` and `unique (circle_id, position)`. A fourth row has no position left, so the table refuses it without a trigger. Every declared Channel is backfilled as position 1. The Channel FK gets its own index (the primary key and the unique both lead on `circle_id`). RLS allows a public read with no write policy, because every Circle-to-Channel write already runs on the service role with authz at the caller (ADR-274). The grant verdict is `public`, since the signed-out Circles index reads it on the session client. `lib/database.types.ts` carries the table by hand, in generated style, until the next regeneration, so `check:schema-contract` sees it.
2. **Position 1 is the primary, and the primary stays on the column.** `writeCircleChannels` (`lib/circles/channel-carriers.ts`) is the only write. It rewrites the join, then sets `circles.topical_channel_id` to position 1. Every reader that asks for "the Channel" (the feed and RLS arms, `public_circle_by_slug`, message rooms, event connectors, market counts, associations, demo) keeps working unchanged.
3. **One vocabulary, pure.** `lib/circles/channels.ts` holds `CIRCLE_MAX_CHANNELS = 3`, the pick check (trim, de-duplicate in picked order, refuse a fourth), the reading of a row primary first (column plus join, so a Circle whose join row is missing still reads its primary), `carriesChannel`, and `anyChannelFilter`. That filter is the column match widened with `id.in.(…)` for Circles carrying the Channel second or third, read from the join at `position > 1`.
4. **The Studio field is a multiselect, not a new kernel kind.** The Circle manifest's `topicalChannelId` reference becomes `channelIds`: `kind: 'multiselect'` with `optionsFrom: 'channels'`. The kernel has no per-field maximum, so the cap is the constant, held by the control, by `setCircleChannels` and by the table. The settings rail lists the chosen Channels (the first marked Primary), removes any of them, and offers a Pillar-grouped select that adds one more until there are three. Refusals land next to the picker and the pick rolls back.
5. **The doors.**
   - `setCircleChannels` refuses a fourth, a missing Circle, a missing Channel, and a paused Channel the Circle does not already carry. A Circle already in a paused Program keeps it when its Host edits the other Channels (the retire switch stops new Circles, ADR-865).
   - `startChapter` and the Space Program flagship put the Program's Channel first. At three, the last one is dropped so the stamp always lands.
   - The Channel Manage hub adds a Circle as one more Channel, and refuses a full Circle with its own notice. Removing a Channel moves the next one up.
   - Deleting a Channel (the `delete_topical_channel` RPC) cascades the join and sets the column to null, so the action reads the Circles whose primary it was first, then promotes each one's next Channel.
6. **Readers that match any of a Circle's Channels:** the Circles index Interest filter and chip counts, the Channel page Circles directory, `listChapters`, the Channels list counts, the Manage hub list, stat and picker, and the Channel insights count. Everything else stays on the primary.
7. **The feed and RLS stay on the primary, by ruling.** Widening the posts read policy, `scoped_feed_for_viewer` and `feed_for_viewer` to match the join is `LIVE-730`, filed parked to 2027-01-05 with the exact predicates. The settings hint says so honestly: "Its posts join the first one's feed."

**Rejected.**
- An array column on `circles`: it has no foreign key, and a deleted Channel would leave dangling ids.
- Restoring `circle_topics` under its old name: it had no cap and no order.
- A trigger to hold the cap or sync the column: the position check plus the unique holds the cap, and one write door keeps the column.
- An RPC to make the rewrite atomic: that is more migration surface than the ruling asked for. The write puts the previous list back when an insert fails, and moves the column last.
- Widening RLS now: the owner ruled "Phase it".

**Consequences.** A Circle shows up on up to three Channel pages and under up to three Interest chips, and counts toward each. Until `LIVE-730` ships, a member tuned into only a Circle's second or third Channel finds the Circle but does not see its cluster posts in their feed. The code must not reach production before the migration: the readers embed and filter `circle_channels`. The orchestrator applies `20270345011400` in its serial window right before the push. The row's probe runs the real `lib/circles/channels.ts` and reads the migration, the write and six readers. It fails on `main` (the migration is gone), and all 9 of 9 arm mutations fail it.

**Rows.** `LIVE-666` (closed here). `LIVE-730` (filed parked here).
