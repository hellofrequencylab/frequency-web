## ADR-1614: A paid split order pays each seller by a transfer that is a ledger row first: planned at settle, created once under a key fixed to the row, retried by a reconciler, and reversed from the webhook (LIVE-622)

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-09-29 · `LIVE-622` (child 2 of 4 of `PROG-D8`, closed here) · builds on [ADR-1576](DECISIONS.md) (the funds-flow seam) and [ADR-1565](DECISIONS.md) (the decomposition), amending its §5 on the finance ledger · beside [ADR-1252](DECISIONS.md) (the cron budget) and [ADR-246](DECISIONS.md) (the entity ledger) · numbered 1614 as assigned by the orchestrator of the 2026-09-29 afternoon wave, against main and every open pull request's head

**Context.** After LIVE-621 a cart from two sellers checks out as one charge on the platform carrying `transfer_group` = the order id, and the order records each seller's share in `metadata.split`. The charge pays nobody. Re-tested on main before writing: no file called `stripe.transfers.create`, no table held a transfer, and the webhook acked every `transfer.*` event through its default arm. A destination charge is atomic; N transfers are N calls that each fail on their own, and a retry that is not idempotent pays a seller twice.

**Decision.**

1. **Every transfer a seller is owed is a row before it is money.** `public.commerce_order_transfers` (migration `20270345009800`, not the `9610` the row named; LIVE-621 took 9700 and the orchestrator assigned 9800): one row per seller per order, unique on `(order_id, seller_key)` where `seller_key` is the seam's `sellerKey`, so two sellers sharing one connected account (a person and the Space they own) stay two rows. States `planned`, `created`, `failed`, `reversed`; a CHECK ties `created` and `reversed` to a `stripe_transfer_id` (unique) and the other two to none. Service role only: RLS on, no policy, revoked from anon and authenticated, like `financial_transactions`.
2. **Planned at settle, from the shares as priced.** `recordCommerceOrderFromSession` hands the separate row it flipped to `settleSplitOrderTransfers`, which never throws. The pure `planTransferShares` writes one share per `metadata.split` entry, gross minus fee, and refuses the whole order unless the gross sums to the order amount and the fees to its platform fee: a transfer is never made on arithmetic the charge does not back. A share whose fee is its whole gross gets no row.
3. **Never twice.** A row is claimed with a compare-and-set on `attempts` and a not-yet-landed status, so two workers cannot both call Stripe for it. The create carries `transfer_group`, `source_transaction` (the order's charge, so it lands before the funds settle), the row id in `metadata`, and the idempotency key `transfer:<row id>`, the same on every attempt. Before a retry creates anything it looks in the order's transfer group for a transfer carrying its row id and adopts it, which covers a retry after Stripe's 24 hour key window and a create that landed with its answer lost; if it cannot look, the attempt fails rather than risk a second transfer. A seller is paid only while the order is paid or fulfilled with no refund.
4. **Not a second entry in the finance ledger (amends ADR-1565 §5).** ADR-1565 said this child ledgers a `payout` row per transfer. It does not. A split order's `financial_transactions` row is its platform fee, exactly as a destination order records its application fee, because the sellers' gross was never platform revenue. A `payout` row for each share against that fee-only row would drive the Labs total negative by every seller's share. The transfer table is the record of money leaving; the finance dashboard's numbers stay revenue.
5. **A reconciler goes back for what did not land.** `/api/cron/reconcile-transfers`, at 20 and 50 past each hour, budget 100: plans a paid split order from the last three days that has no row (the settle died between the flip and the plan), retries planned and failed rows untouched for ten minutes under 8 attempts, and logs every row over the ceiling as `commerce.transfer.stuck`, one line each, every run. It is unmonitored in `lib/observability/slos.ts` with its reason (a Healthchecks check is an account change, and the account holds 20).
6. **The webhook reads both transfer events.** `transfer.created` adopts a transfer whose write here was lost; `transfer.reversed` records Stripe's cumulative `amount_reversed`, monotonic so a replay converges, and marks the row `reversed` once it is all back (a partial reversal stays `created`). Both fire on the platform account, so the platform-scoped Stripe destination must subscribe to them.
7. **The surface for the next two rows is `lib/commerce/transfers.ts`.** `listOrderTransfers(orderId)` is the read a split refund reverses from (LIVE-623) and a seller's share is shown from (LIVE-624); an operator retry resets `attempts` on a stuck row and the reconciler takes it.

**Rejected.** Creating the transfers without a row first (a failure between two of N calls leaves nothing to reconcile from). A random or per-attempt idempotency key (a retry is then a new transfer). Relying on the idempotency key alone (it expires after 24 hours, and the reconciler runs for longer than that). Keying a row by connected account (two sellers can share one). A `payout` row in `financial_transactions` per transfer (point 4). A member read policy on the ledger now (the seller view is LIVE-624's, and a money table stays service role until a surface needs more).

**Consequences.** A paid split order now pays each seller, and a transfer that does not land is visible as a row, retried, and logged when it stops being retried. Until LIVE-623 lands a refund of a split order comes from the platform balance while the sellers keep their transfers; a transfer not yet created is held for a refunded order. The migration is authored only; the orchestrator applies it before this change merges. The liability position is unchanged: the platform is merchant of record, and money can already sit in a seller's balance when a chargeback lands, which is what LIVE-623 reverses.

**Rows.** `LIVE-622` (closed here). `PROG-D8` keeps its open arms (LIVE-623, LIVE-624). `LIVE-623`, `LIVE-624` untouched.
