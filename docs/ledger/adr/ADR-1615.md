## ADR-1615: A refund of a split order comes back from every seller pro rata: each transfer is reversed by its share of the gross, once, after the buyer is refunded, and a transfer never made is cancelled (LIVE-623)

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-09-29 · `LIVE-623` (child 3 of 4 of `PROG-D8`, closed here) · builds on [ADR-1614](DECISIONS.md) (the transfer ledger), amending its §3 on a partly refunded order, and [ADR-1565](DECISIONS.md) §3 (the pro rata rule, built as written) · beside [ADR-1576](DECISIONS.md) §6 (a separate refund sends no transfer flag) · numbered 1615 as assigned by the orchestrator of the 2026-09-29 afternoon wave, against main and every open pull request's head

**Context.** After LIVE-622 a split order pays each seller by a transfer after the charge. Re-tested before writing, on the LIVE-622 head: `refundCommerceOrder` refunded a separate order on the PaymentIntent with no transfer flag (correct, there is none on the charge) and nothing else, so the platform balance paid the whole refund while every seller kept their transfer; the webhook's `charge.refunded` reached the same recorders and nothing more; no file called `stripe.transfers.createReversal`; and the webhook acked `charge.dispute.closed` through its default arm, so a lost chargeback on any commerce order was reconciled by nobody. ADR-1565 §3 had already ruled whose share a partial comes from: every seller, pro rata to their share of the gross, the fee reduced in the same proportion.

**Decision.**

1. **One pure proportion, `proportionRefund` in `lib/commerce/split-refund.ts`.** A refund is divided across the seller shares by gross, largest-remainder rounded (floors first, the leftover cents to the largest remainders, ties to the larger share then list order), so the shares sum exactly to the refund. Each share then splits transfer:gross into the seller's reversal (floored, so a rounding cent is the platform's and never taken from a seller) and the platform's fee part. A full refund reverses each transfer exactly; a reversal never exceeds its transfer. A share whose fee took its whole gross has no row and its part stays with the platform, which kept it.
2. **The refund writes a target before any money moves.** `commerce_order_transfers.refund_reversal_cents` (migration `20270345009850`, authored only) is the cumulative amount a row owes back, only ever raised, never above the transfer; `reversal_owed_cents` is generated as the target minus `reversed_cents`, floored at zero, and is the reconciler's queue. `reverseSplitTransfers(orderId, refunded)` takes the CUMULATIVE amount refunded (a charge's `amount_refunded`, or that plus a lost dispute), so a partial and its later top-up each raise the targets and a replay raises nothing.
3. **One reversal per owed row, never twice, never too much.** The row is claimed with a compare-and-set on `reversal_attempts`; Stripe is asked how much of the transfer is already reversed (`amount_reversed`, which counts a lost write and a dashboard reversal); only target minus that is reversed, under the idempotency key `transfer-reversal:<row>:<from>-<to>`. The key names the refund by the cents it moves the transfer from and to, not by a Stripe refund id, because the three callers of one refund must derive the same key and only this is common to them: the refund action has a refund id, its `charge.refunded` payload carries the cumulative `amount_refunded` and no refund list on this API version, and a lost dispute has no refund at all. When Stripe cannot be asked, nothing is reversed and the row is failed.
4. **The buyer first.** `refundCommerceOrder` branches on the funds flow: a destination order keeps `reverse_transfer` + `refund_application_fee`, untouched; a separate order refunds on the PaymentIntent with neither flag, records the refund, then reverses, and a reversal that throws never fails the refund. `recordCommerceRefundFromCharge` does the same for a refund made anywhere (and throws on a database error so the webhook redelivers). A failed reversal is recorded on its row (`last_error`, `reversal_attempts`) and stops no other seller.
5. **A transfer never made is cancelled, a partly refunded order still pays (amends ADR-1614 §3).** A new status `cancelled`: a full refund cancels every planned or failed row, and the executor cancels them itself if it meets a refunded order first. A cancelled row carries its whole amount as the target, so a transfer that was already at Stripe when the cancel ran is adopted as created when it lands (`markCreated` and `transfer.created` accept a cancelled row) and then reversed in full. ADR-1614 held every row of an order with any refund; a partial refund leaves the order paid (the schema has no partial state), so its sellers are now paid their share and the refund's part of it is reversed after, from the target the refund wrote first. A partial refund of an order the settle never planned plans it first, so the share is still owed back.
6. **The reconciler takes what did not land.** `/api/cron/reconcile-transfers` runs `reconcileSplitReversals` after the transfer pass, on the same budget: created rows still owed, under 8 attempts and untouched for ten minutes, grouped by order; rows over the ceiling log `commerce.transfer.reversal_stuck`, one line each, every run.
7. **A lost dispute is a refund of the disputed amount.** `charge.dispute.closed` with status `lost` on a commerce charge records the order refunded for the refunds already on the charge plus the disputed amount, through the same recorder, and on a split order reverses through the same function: the money that already sat in the sellers' balances comes back the way a refund would. Won and warning-closed disputes change nothing. The platform-scoped Stripe destination must subscribe to `charge.dispute.closed`.
8. **Not a finance row per reversal (per ADR-1614 §4).** The platform's revenue on a split order is its fee, and the existing recorder already reverses that fee pro rata on a partial and in full on a full refund; a seller's transfer was never platform revenue, so its reversal is recorded on the transfer row only. The row's item 5 ("a `refund` row against the `payout` row it undoes") has no payout row to stand against.

**Rejected.** Reversing from one seller, or the largest (the rule is pro rata, ADR-1565 §3). A key per Stripe refund id (the webhook payload does not carry it and a dispute has none, so two callers of one refund would make two reversals). A reversal amount computed from our own `reversed_cents` (a lost write or a dashboard reversal would be reversed again). Holding a partly refunded order's unpaid transfer forever (the seller is owed the rest of their share). Deleting a planned row on a full refund (the ledger is the record; a cancel is a state). Reversing a destination order's one transfer after a lost dispute here (its unwind is its own flag set; it is recorded refunded, and the seller-side recovery is a later row if one is needed).

**Consequences.** A refund of a split order, full or partial, from the product or the dashboard, and a lost chargeback, now come back from each seller in proportion, once, and what cannot come back yet is a row that is retried and then logged. The migration is authored only; the orchestrator applies it before this change merges. The liability position is unchanged: the platform is merchant of record, and a seller whose balance is empty leaves a reversal owed that the platform carries until it lands. The seller and operator surfaces for all of this are LIVE-624's.
