Imagery · the library

Every photograph is a real Frequency gathering, shot at golden hour in North County. That is the whole rule, and it is why the site can carry type over pictures without either fighting the other: the light is already warm, so the amber has somewhere to sit. Frames are catalogued here by the job they do — a wide hero and a portrait crop are not interchangeable, and each ships at the size its slot needs.

Try it

Wide · heroes and full-bleed beats

Landscape, room-scale, with air above the crowd for the headline to land in. 1600 × 1066 source; the -card crop is 1000 × 750 for a figure slot.

Mid-page beats

One sentence over a photograph, seamed top and bottom. The alternative to a slat band when the picture is the argument — never both on the same page.

Portrait · tall crops

For a rail module, a story crop, or an aside beside a hero. 900 × 1600 source. Vertical frames carry one or two people; a room-scale shot cropped tall loses the room and the point.

The Lab · interiors

The physical-space reference: black wood slats, amber strip lighting, heat and cold. The only frames where the room is the subject and nobody is in it.

Choosing a frame

Prefer
  • Backs of heads, mid-laugh, mid-motion. Nobody looking at the lens.
  • Enough of the room to tell you where you are.
  • Golden hour, or amber light indoors.
  • Air above the crowd, if type is going on it.
Avoid
  • Anything posed at the camera, or shot in flat midday light.
  • Illustration and 3D render — this brand has no drawn imagery.
  • Stock: strangers in an office, a laughing salad, a lens flare on white.
  • A face cropped by a headline. Move the type, not the crop.

Real members, real rooms. If it did not happen at a Frequency gathering, it does not go on the site.

Golden hour is the palette. The warm light is why amber type sits on these frames at all — cool photography breaks the whole system.

The job decides the crop. Wide for heroes, -card for figure slots, -tall for rails. Never re-crop a room-scale shot into a portrait.

Name by subject, suffix by role. closing-circle.jpg, closing-circle-card.jpg. A page never guesses which file it wants.

Never a photograph behind a photograph. One picture per band, and a band with a picture does not also get the slat ground.

Caption in a gradient, not a shadow. A warm ink gradient holds at any brightness; a text-shadow only survives on a light frame.
